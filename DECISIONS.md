# Decision log

Short notes on decisions that affected the build, oldest first, newest at the bottom. Each entry starts with the date and time it was recorded, in UTC, taken from the commit that added it. Assumptions made where the brief was silent are marked **Assumption**.

- 2026-11-11 15:52 · **Track 1, policy administration.** Fullstack TypeScript: Next.js (App Router), Prisma, Postgres. One codebase keeps the frontend and backend types in sync, and Postgres gives us real transactions for financial writes.
- 2026-11-11 15:52 · **One product with flat per-coverage pricing.** A Small Business Package with General Liability ($1,095/yr, required), Cyber ($730/yr), and Business Property ($365/yr). The prices are chosen to work out to whole dollars per day so the math is easy to verify live. **Assumption:** no rating factors, taxes, or fees.
- 2026-11-11 15:52 · **Money in integer cents, rounded per line.** Each pro-rated line is rounded half-up to the cent, and totals are sums of rounded lines. This avoids floating-point drift and makes every statement line reproducible by hand.
- 2026-11-11 15:52 · **Daily pro-rata with actual days in term.** `annual × days covered ÷ days in term`, start date inclusive, end date exclusive. **Assumption:** the brief doesn't specify a convention, and this is the common industry one.
- 2026-11-11 15:52 · **Append-only ledger, and corrections reverse and rebook.** No ledger row is ever edited or deleted. A correction posts a reversal of the original charge and a new charge, so the brief's requirement to keep "the original charge and the correction visible" falls out of the design rather than being a special case.
- 2026-11-11 15:52 · **Cancellation is pro-rata with no penalty.** Refund equals paid minus earned premium, sent back through Stripe Refunds. **Assumption:** no short-rate penalty or minimum earned premium.
- 2026-11-11 15:52 · **Payment status comes only from Stripe webhooks.** The browser redirect is never trusted to mark a payment as paid. This is what makes "pending, succeeded, failed" honest.
- 2026-11-11 15:52 · **Integrations: Stripe test mode (live) and Resend email (live, sandbox).** *(Superseded below by DocuSign.)* Carrier, underwriting, and policy documents are simulated. Every integration call and webhook is stored in an integration log for evidence.
- 2026-11-11 15:52 · **Left out: installments.** Pay-in-full only. This keeps "amount due" a single number per policy, and installments wouldn't exercise anything new in the pro-rata logic.
- 2026-11-11 15:58 · **Credits are applied first, then refunded automatically.** When a correction or other change leaves the customer with a credit (a "return premium"), the credit first pays down any open balance on the policy. Anything left is refunded to the original card through Stripe without the broker having to act. This matches how carrier billing systems handle return premium, and it avoids leaving the customer's money sitting idle. **Assumption:** we have no minimum refund threshold, although real systems often hold very small credits.
- 2026-11-11 15:58 · **Hosting and services on free tiers only.** Vercel Hobby, Neon free Postgres, Stripe test mode, and DocuSign developer sandbox. None of them needs a credit card.
- 2026-11-11 16:07 · **Second integration is DocuSign, not Resend.** The policyholder e-signs the application (embedded signing in our app) before the broker can bind the policy, and a DocuSign Connect webhook records completion. We chose it over email because it belongs to the insurance workflow (signed application before binding) rather than being generic plumbing. The cost is about 2 to 3 hours of setup (JWT auth, document tabs, webhook verification). Email notifications are left out and can be added later.
- 2026-11-11 16:29 · **Brokers edit policies only through dated changes (endorsements).** Brokers can edit coverage, correct any past change (including the original issue), and edit non-money details. Every edit is a new history entry, and money-affecting edits post ledger lines rather than overwriting anything, so each amount stays traceable. Manual premium overrides and term extensions are left out because they add rules without exercising new money logic.
- 2026-11-11 16:31 · **One "Edit coverage" action instead of separate add and remove.** The broker picks an effective date and turns coverages on or off in a single change. One change can post several ledger lines (a charge per coverage added, a credit per coverage removed), all tied to the same history entry. This matches how a broker thinks about it ("change the coverage from this date") and keeps the history to one entry per decision.
- 2026-11-11 16:56 · **Next.js Cache Components turned off.** The Next.js 16 starter enables Cache Components, which prerenders and caches parts of pages. Every page in this app is per-user financial data that must be fresh on each request, so caching adds risk and no benefit. All pages render on demand.
- 2026-11-11 16:56 · **Prisma pinned to 7.10.1.** npm's `latest` tag for the Prisma CLI currently points at an 8.0 release candidate. We pinned the CLI to the stable 7.x line that matches `@prisma/client`.
- 2026-11-11 16:56 · **Demo auth is a signed cookie with seeded users.** Sessions are an HS256-signed, httpOnly cookie, and passwords are bcrypt hashes. There's no sign-up, password reset, or third-party identity provider, because reviewers only need two demo logins. **Assumption:** each policyholder login belongs to exactly one customer.
- 2026-11-11 16:56 · **Plain Tailwind instead of a component library.** The plan mentioned shadcn/ui. We're using hand-written Tailwind components instead, to keep the dependency list small.
- 2026-11-11 16:59 · **Premium is always calculated by replaying the policy's history.** One pure function (`src/lib/engine/replay.ts`) applies the issue, coverage changes, and cancellation in order and returns every premium line. A correction replays the history with the corrected action in place of the original, compares the result with what's on the statement, and posts only the reversals and new lines needed to close the gap. This means a correction can never disagree with the original calculation, a correction that would break a later change is refused with a clear message, and the same action can be corrected more than once.
- 2026-11-11 16:59 · **Fixed 12-month term.** The broker chooses the start date and the end date is one year later. "Days in term" is therefore 365, or 366 if the term includes Feb 29, and a full term always costs exactly the annual premium. **Assumption:** short-term policies aren't needed for the demo.
- 2026-11-11 16:59 · **Refunds go back against the newest card payment first.** A card refund has to be tied to a specific payment and can't exceed what's left of it, so a large refund can be split across several payments, newest to oldest.
- 2026-11-11 17:06 · **Every new customer gets a policyholder login automatically.** Creating a customer also creates a login with the customer's email and the shared demo password, so a reviewer can create a customer as the broker and immediately sign in as that policyholder. A real platform would send an invitation instead.
- 2026-11-11 17:06 · **Corrections can change the policy start date.** Correcting the original issue to a different start date shifts the whole 12-month term, so every premium line is reversed and rebooked against the new term. The same replay-and-compare code handles it with no special case.
- 2026-11-11 17:13 · **Payment status is decided by Stripe's servers, through two doors.** The webhook is the main one. As a backstop, opening a policy page asks Stripe directly about any payment or refund that is still unsettled, so a missed or delayed webhook can't leave a payment stuck. Both paths run the same idempotent code, so hearing about a payment twice posts one statement line. The browser is never trusted to say a payment succeeded. This refines the earlier "webhooks only" decision.
- 2026-11-11 17:13 · **Four payment states.** "Not submitted" (the payment form was opened), pending (Stripe is processing), succeeded, and failed. A failed attempt keeps its own record, and its Stripe PaymentIntent is cancelled before a new attempt starts, so an old attempt can never complete on top of a new one.
- 2026-11-11 17:13 · **A payment always covers the full amount due.** No partial payments, in keeping with pay-in-full. If the amount due changes while a payment form is open, the unfinished PaymentIntent is updated to the new amount.
- 2026-11-11 17:13 · **Refunds reach the statement only when Stripe confirms them.** Until then the statement shows a credit and the payments table shows the refund as pending. If Stripe rejects a refund, the credit stays visible and the broker gets a "Refund credit" button to retry.
- 2026-11-11 19:26 · **Card and US bank account only.** Stripe's form would otherwise also offer Cash App, Amazon Pay, and Klarna, which don't fit paying a commercial insurance premium. Cards settle immediately, and bank debits give a genuine pending state before they clear.
- 2026-11-11 20:34 · **Customer details are edited in place, with a history entry on every policy.** Name, contact, and address don't affect any amount, so the customer record is updated directly. Each change is written to the history of every policy the customer holds (field, old value, new value, who, when). Changing the contact email doesn't change the policyholder's login.
- 2026-11-11 20:34 · **Corrections explain themselves in plain English.** Each correction's history entry says how many days changed, the daily rate, the old and new premium, and the net result, built from the same statement lines the customer sees. The debrief asks what the customer sees, and this is the answer.
- 2026-11-11 20:37 · **A signed application comes before the policy.** With DocuSign configured, the broker sends an application, the policyholder signs it inside the app (DocuSign embedded signing), and only then can the broker issue the policy. The signed PDF, with DocuSign's certificate of completion, is linked from the policy and the customer page. The application isn't part of the ledger: no money is owed until the policy is issued. If DocuSign isn't configured (local development without keys), the broker issues directly.
- 2026-11-11 20:37 · **"Signed" is decided by DocuSign's servers.** Like payments, there are two triggers, a per-envelope webhook and the policyholder's return from the signing screen, and both only cause a fresh status check against DocuSign's API. The webhook body is never trusted, so it needs no shared secret: a forged call can do nothing but trigger a status check.
- 2026-11-11 20:37 · **DocuSign through its REST API directly, not the SDK.** Four calls (create envelope, signing link, status, download) plus the JWT sign-in are about 200 lines with no extra dependency. The code refuses to run against anything but DocuSign's sandbox hosts, as the Stripe code refuses live keys.
- 2026-11-11 21:50 · **Claims, renewals, and broker commissions are each built in their simplest honest form.** The brief names them as extensions once the core workflow works.
- 2026-11-11 21:50 · **Commission is derived, not stored.** The broker earns a flat 10% of premium. Commission is calculated from the premium lines on each policy's statement, so every dollar of commission points at the statement line behind it and the two can't disagree. A credit or reversal earns negative commission, which is the clawback when premium is returned. Rounding is to the cent with halves away from zero, so reversing a line always reverses its commission exactly. The rate is saved on the policy at issue so a later rate change wouldn't rewrite the past. **Assumptions:** commission is earned when premium is charged, not when it's collected, and paying the broker isn't modelled.
- 2026-11-11 21:50 · **A renewal is a new policy, linked to the old one.** It covers the 12 months after the current term, carries forward the coverages in force on the last day of that term (a coverage removed mid-term isn't renewed), and is priced from today's rate table. Giving it its own number, statement, and payments keeps each term's money traceable separately. A policy can be renewed once, and a cancelled policy can't be renewed. **Assumptions:** a renewal is on terms the customer already signed, so it needs no new application, and later changes to the expiring policy don't flow into a renewal that already exists.
- 2026-11-11 21:50 · **Claims are kept off the premium statement.** Claim money flows from the insurer to the customer, while the statement tracks what the customer owes in premium. Mixing them would make "amount due" wrong. Each claim has its own traceable record: who filed it, who decided it, when it was paid, and how the payout was calculated.
- 2026-11-11 21:50 · **A claim is covered only if its coverage was in force on the date of loss,** judged by the policy's history as it stands now. This ties claims to the same effective dates the reviewers' scenario corrects: moving a coverage's start date earlier can bring a loss into cover, and a claim whose coverage is later corrected or cancelled away from its loss date is flagged and can't be approved.
- 2026-11-11 21:50 · **Payout = approved loss − $500 deductible, capped at the coverage's per-claim limit.** The deductible and limit are saved on the claim when it's approved. **Assumptions:** one flat deductible, fixed per-claim limits with no annual aggregate, and no partial payments or reserves.
- 2026-11-11 21:50 · **Claim payments are simulated.** Marking a claim paid records the payment in the app. No money moves, because paying a customer would need a payout integration that the brief doesn't ask for.
- 2026-11-11 21:50 · **Integration tests run against a separate local test database.** They exercise the real service code, including the reviewers' scenario, without Stripe or DocuSign. The test setup refuses to run against any database that isn't local.
- 2026-11-12 07:59 · **Premium is due on the term's start date.** A policy whose term hasn't started shows "due by" its start date in place of a bare "amount due", and can be paid early. This matters for renewals, which the broker can create well ahead of the new term. **Simplifications:** a real system would only offer renewal 60 to 90 days before expiry, send a renewal notice, and cancel an unpaid renewal back to its start date after a grace period. We let the broker renew at any time so the flow is easy to try, and leave out notices, grace periods, and lapse for non-payment.
- 2026-11-12 08:29 · **A credit is also refunded when a payment succeeds, not only after a broker's change.** The amount due can drop while a payment is still clearing (a bank debit takes days). When that payment lands for the old, higher amount, the extra is refunded straight away, by the same refund code a correction uses. Before this, the credit waited for the broker to press "Retry refund", which contradicted "refunded automatically" above.
- 2026-11-12 08:29 · **An open payment form follows the amount due down, never up.** If a broker's change lowers the amount due while the customer has the payment form open, the unfinished Stripe payment is lowered to match, or cancelled if nothing is due. If the change raises the amount due, the open payment is left alone: the customer agreed to the amount on their screen, so they pay that and the rest stays due. This is the one case where a payment covers less than the full amount due.
- 2026-11-12 08:29 · **Policy numbers come from a database sequence.** They used to be "number of policies, plus one", which gives two policies issued at the same moment the same number. A Postgres sequence can't repeat. **Trade-off:** a number is used up even if issuing fails, so numbers are unique but can have gaps.
- 2026-11-12 08:29 · **Payment tests use a stand-in for Stripe.** The refund and open-payment rules are tested against the real database with a fake Stripe client that records what it was asked to do, so the tests make no network calls.
- 2026-11-12 08:58 · **A policy and its renewal never overlap, and a gap between them is only allowed on purpose.** An overlap would charge the customer twice for the same days and put a claim under two policies, so it is always refused. A gap (days with no cover) does happen legitimately, for example after a lapse, so the broker can choose one, but only with a written reason. The gap is then shown on both policies and noted in both histories.
- 2026-11-12 08:58 · **A renewal follows the policy it renews by default.** When a correction moves the end of a policy's cover, its renewal is moved to start that day, and the renewal's own renewal follows in turn. When a renewed policy is cancelled, the renewal is cancelled from its first day, which credits everything charged on it. Both policies are saved together or not at all, and each keeps its own history entry and statement lines. The broker can instead leave the renewal where it is, if that leaves a gap rather than an overlap. If the renewal can neither follow (for example, one of its own changes would fall outside its new term) nor stay, the change is refused. This replaces the earlier assumption that changes to an expiring policy never reach its renewal. Coverage changes still don't.
- 2026-11-12 08:58 · **A renewal's own start date can't move into the term it renews.** Moving it later is a gap and needs a reason. To move both policies, the broker corrects the earlier one.
- 2026-11-12 08:58 · **History notes.** A new history entry type, "Note", records a fact that changes no amount. It is used for coverage gaps.
- 2026-11-12 09:51 · **A read-only window onto the deployed database.** `npm run prod:query` lets us confirm what happened in production (a signature, a payment, a refund) without relying on screenshots. The connection string lives in a git-ignored local file and is never printed. The script can't change data: the session is set read-only, each query runs in a read-only transaction that is rolled back, and queries are sent as prepared statements so only one statement is accepted.
- 2026-11-12 10:24 · **Buttons keep their size and wording while they work.** A busy button used to swap its text for something like "Saving…", which changed its width and nudged the layout. Now the label keeps its space, a spinner shows in its place, and the button is disabled so it can't be clicked twice. One shared component does this for every button, and a browser test checks the size doesn't change.
- 2026-11-12 10:24 · **Three layers of automated tests.** `npm test` covers the money rules and the workflow against a local test database. `npm run test:stripe` runs the real payment and refund code against Stripe's test mode through its API. `npm run test:e2e` drives the reviewers' scenario in a real browser, typing test cards into Stripe's own card form. DocuSign signing stays a manual check, because it happens on DocuSign's page.
- 2026-11-12 11:15 · **The broker's "Issue policy" step stays.** After the policyholder signs, the broker still clicks to issue. We considered issuing automatically on signature, which is how fixed-price online products work, and kept the explicit step because it mirrors binding in commercial insurance: a signed application is an offer, and coverage starts when someone with binding authority accepts it.
- 2026-11-12 11:15 · **Stripe's Link wallet is switched off in the payment form.** Link added a "Save my information" section and offered Klarna through it, neither of which fits paying an insurance premium. The form now shows only card and US bank account.
- 2026-11-12 11:25 · **The Stripe webhook ignores events for policies it doesn't know.** One Stripe test account serves the deployed app, local development, and the automated tests, and Stripe delivers every event to the deployed app's address. Events from the other copies were showing up in the deployed integration log. The handler now checks the policy id that we attach to every payment and refund, and neither acts on nor logs events for policies that aren't in its own database.
- 2026-11-12 11:25 · **Refunds and claims are checked on the deployed app by a script.** `npm run test:deployed` runs a correction that triggers a refund, and a paid and a denied claim, in a browser against the deployed site. The results are then confirmed from the deployed database and from Stripe's own records.
- 2026-11-12 11:47 · **A "Load demo scenario" button builds the reviewers' starting point.** One click creates a new fictional customer whose policy was issued and paid six weeks ago, with Cyber added and paid three weeks ago but recorded as starting a week later than it did. It uses the app's normal issue and coverage-change code and two real Stripe test payments, so a later correction can refund to a card. Two shortcuts are taken, and the policy page states both: the DocuSign signature is skipped, and the app's records up to that point are backdated, while Stripe's records show the payments as made at load time. This is the only place records are re-dated, it never touches an amount, and it applies only to data the button has just created.
- 2026-11-12 12:07 · **A claim doesn't depend on the premium being paid.** A claim can be filed and approved while premium is outstanding, because in insurance cover stays in force until the policy is actually cancelled; an insurer would normally either net the unpaid premium off the claim payment or cancel for non-payment after notice. We build neither. The broker is shown the unpaid amount beside the claims they are deciding, and the simplification is listed in the README. **Assumption:** this is the honest minimal version; blocking claims on unpaid policies would be wrong, not just simpler.
- 2026-11-12 12:07 · **Cancellation, refund, and renewal are checked on the deployed app by a script.** `npm run test:deployed -- cancel-and-renew` loads two demo scenarios on the deployed site, cancels one (checking the unused premium is refunded across both card payments) and renews the other (checking the link between the two policies, the due date, and an early payment).
- 2026-11-12 12:24 · **Leftover test data on the deployed app is removed by policy, not by editing.** Before taking evidence, we cleared the debris of testing from the deployed database: one policy whose claims and correction were test probes, and integration log rows for Stripe events that belonged to other copies of the app. The cleanup removes a policy whole, with everything attached, or not at all, and never edits part of a policy's history. It refuses a policy tied to a signed application or a renewal, previews by default, and needs a typed confirmation to delete. We chose this over a full reset because it keeps the DocuSign-signed policy and the cancellation and renewal already verified there.
- 2026-11-12 14:42 · **Commission tables show dates.** The Commissions page shows each policy's term, and the cancellation date if it has one, so a policy and its renewal can be told apart and a low figure on a cancelled policy explains itself. The commission table on a policy shows the date each line was posted, so a clawback is dated when the premium was returned, not when it was first charged. We stopped there: no paid date and no filtering by period, because paying commission out isn't modelled and those would suggest it is.
- 2026-11-12 14:51 · **The policyholder is told why an amount is due, beside the Pay button.** After a correction the policyholder saw "Pay $14.00" at the top of the page while the explanation sat at the bottom, in the history. The pay card now carries a "Why this is due" note with the same wording, the broker's reason, and a link to the history entry. The rule for what it lists: payments settle the oldest premium first, so whatever is still owed belongs to the newest changes, and the note walks back from the newest change until the changes account for the balance. That is usually one entry. It needs no record of what the customer has already read, and it goes away when the amount is paid. We left out emails and notifications. A change that lowers the amount and is refunded straight away leaves nothing to pay, so it shows no note; the statement and history explain it.
- 2026-11-12 14:51 · **A summary row above the statement.** Four figures in one row: premium charged (after corrections and credits), less payments, plus refunds, equals the amount due. They are sums of the statement's own lines, so they can't disagree with it. We left out earned versus unearned premium and exports.
- 2026-11-12 15:37 · **The Pay button shows the amount Stripe will collect.** The policyholder's page can be open while the broker changes the policy, so the amount on the page can be out of date. The pay form now takes its amount from the Stripe payment itself, not from the page, and checks it again with Stripe at the moment Pay is pressed. If it differs from the amount the customer was last shown, in either direction, nothing is charged: the form says the amount was X and is now Y, and the customer has to tick a box agreeing to the new amount before the button works.
- 2026-11-12 15:37 · **A refund row is the refund, and it is resumed until its outcome is known.** A refund is saved in our database before Stripe is contacted, so that two runs can't refund the same credit. Two interruptions used to go wrong: if the app stopped before reaching Stripe, the saved row held the credit forever; and if Stripe made the refund but its answer was lost, the row was marked failed and a retry could refund the same credit again. Now only a clear refusal from Stripe marks a refund failed. Anything else leaves the row waiting, and it is sent again with the same idempotency key whenever the policy is opened or the broker presses Retry refund. Before resending, Stripe is asked whether it already has a refund carrying that row's reference, which covers a resume after Stripe's 24-hour idempotency window. **Assumption:** a Stripe server error that Stripe replays for the same key would keep a refund waiting until the key expires; we accept that for a demo.
- 2026-11-12 15:37 · **Issuing from a signed application is one transaction with a guarded status change.** The policy and its premium charge used to be saved before the application was marked issued, so two brokers clicking at once, or a retry after a failure in between, could issue two policies from one signature. Now the application is first claimed (moved from signed to issued, only if it is still signed), then the policy is created and linked, all in one database transaction. The second caller waits on the first and is told the application has already been issued. If creating the policy fails, the claim is undone with it.
- 2026-11-12 15:37 · **A policy has one open payment attempt, reserved before Stripe is asked.** Two checkouts opened at the same moment (two tabs, or a double click) could each create a payment and a Stripe PaymentIntent and both collect the balance. The attempt is now found or created under a lock on the policy, and the PaymentIntent is created with the attempt's id as its idempotency key, so concurrent requests share one attempt and one PaymentIntent. An attempt that was reserved but never reached Stripe is picked up the next time the form is opened. We chose the lock over a database uniqueness rule, because a declined attempt can legitimately come back to life while a newer one is open.
- 2026-11-12 15:37 · **A broker's confirmation carries the preview it confirms.** A preview and its confirmation are two requests, and a payment or another change can land between them. A cancellation previewed as leaving an amount due could then be saved as one that sends a refund. Each preview now has a fingerprint of every amount shown (each line, and each policy's balance before and after). The confirmation sends it back, and under the policy lock the change is recalculated and saved only if the fingerprint still matches. If it doesn't, nothing is saved and the broker sees the new figures with a notice, and confirms again.
- 2026-11-12 15:57 · **An application's status only moves forward, and each move is checked in the write itself.** A status check reads the application, waits for DocuSign, and then writes. A slow answer could arrive after another request had marked the application signed and the broker had issued the policy, and the late write set it back to signed, ready to be issued again. Every status write now applies only to an application still in the state it expects (awaiting signature for signed, voided or withdrawn; signed with no policy for issued). This closes the second way one signature could issue two policies.
- 2026-11-12 15:57 · **The "Why this is due" rows always add up to the amount on the Pay button.** The first version stopped at the newest change large enough to cover the balance, so it could show +$28.00 beside a $14.00 button when a −$14.00 change came just before. The rule now: walk back from the newest change until the changes come to exactly the amount due, and show those. If no run of recent changes does (a payment covered part of a change), show everything on the statement since the balance was last zero, payments and refunds included. With more than one row, a total is shown.
- 2026-11-12 15:57 · **A correction's explanation counts the days being paid for.** It used to add up the days on every line a correction posted, so on a cancelled policy it added the credited (unused) days to the charged days and could report 549 days on a one-year policy. A charge now adds its days and a credit subtracts them, which gives the days of cover the customer pays for. Where only a credit is involved (correcting the date a coverage was removed), the explanation talks about the days credited.
- 2026-11-12 15:57 · **A renewal is created only if it is still the one the broker was shown.** The Renew button sent only the policy, so a coverage added in another tab after the page loaded produced a renewal at a different premium without asking. The renewal card now carries a fingerprint of its term, coverages and premium, the same idea as for corrections. The renewal is recalculated under the policy lock, and if it differs nothing is created and the card shows the new terms.
- 2026-11-12 15:57 · **A payment's amount only ever goes down, and checkout stops if the amount can't be checked.** An amount already shown on a Pay button is never raised: if more is due when checkout is opened again, the old attempt is cancelled at Stripe and a new one started, so a form left open in another tab can no longer be completed for an amount it doesn't show. And if Stripe can't be asked for the current amount when Pay is pressed, the payment isn't sent.
- 2026-11-12 15:57 · **A completed payment is never downgraded, and that is enforced in the write.** News about a payment can be handled out of order: a "processing" notice after it has succeeded. The check used to be made on a copy read earlier, so a settled bank payment could be set back to pending while its line stayed on the statement, and later checks didn't repair it. The writes now exclude succeeded payments themselves, and recording a success sets the status even when the statement line already exists.
- 2026-11-12 15:57 · **The broker is told whether a refund has completed or is still on its way.** A bank refund takes days, and the broker's notice said "was refunded" as soon as Stripe accepted it, while the statement was still waiting. Completed and pending amounts are now reported separately, and the wording says "original payment method", since a payment can be by bank account as well as card.
- 2026-11-12 20:04 · **A refund that fails after Stripe reported it succeeded puts the customer back in credit.** Stripe can accept a refund, report it succeeded, and change it to failed later when the customer's bank turns it away. The app used to ignore that, leaving the statement at $0.00 with the customer still owed the money. The refund's line now stays on the statement and a second line cancels it out, so the credit is back for the broker to resolve with Retry refund. The second line points at the first, and the database lets a line be cancelled only once, so repeated news posts nothing more. Checked against Stripe's own test card for this case, which flips the refund about two minutes after accepting it. **Assumption:** this relies on Stripe's webhook, which Stripe retries for three days; opening the policy doesn't re-check refunds that have already succeeded.
- 2026-11-12 20:04 · **Bank payments stay in scope, and a payment the bank takes back makes the amount due again.** A bank debit can be returned after it has cleared, and Stripe reports that as a dispute, not as a payment event, so the policy stayed marked as paid. The webhook now handles disputes. Once a dispute is lost the payment's line is kept and a second line cancels it out, exactly once, by the same rule as a failed refund. The policyholder sees the returned payment as the reason the amount is due and can pay again. A returned payment can't be refunded against. **Assumptions:** a returned bank debit is lost from the start, per Stripe's documentation. A card dispute that is still open is not reflected until it is lost. A refund already sent against a payment that is later returned is not untangled. Not tested against Stripe itself, only with a stand-in, because a test-mode bank dispute takes days to arrive.
- 2026-11-12 20:04 · **An amount due below $0.50 stays on the statement and is collected with the next payment.** Stripe won't charge less than $0.50, and a correction across a leap year can leave a few cents due. The app used to open checkout for it, which Stripe then rejected. Now no payment is offered: the policyholder sees "Nothing to pay right now" with the reason, both roles see the amount marked as too small to collect by itself, and an open payment form is closed if a change takes the amount below the minimum. The cents are not written off: they are added to whatever is charged next. **Assumption:** a real system would have a small-balance write-off rule; we carry the balance instead, so no premium disappears without a decision.
- 2026-11-12 20:04 · **The reason saved with a change is the one on screen at confirmation.** The reason field stays editable after the preview, but confirmation sent the request captured when Preview was pressed, so a reason rewritten afterwards was lost from the history. The flow now keeps the latest reason separately and sends that one, including a reason typed while the preview is still loading. The same fix covers editing coverage and cancelling.
- 2026-11-12 20:04 · **A claim's decision is written only if it is still undecided, and claim numbers come from a database sequence.** Two brokers deciding the same claim at once could both be told they had succeeded, with different outcomes. The check for "still submitted" is now part of the write, so only one decision commits and the other broker is told it has already been decided. Claim numbers were the count of claims plus one, so claims filed at the same moment could pick the same number and one filing failed. They now come from a Postgres sequence, as policy numbers do; numbers are unique but can have gaps.
- 2026-11-12 22:04 · **Each bank return is its own record, keyed by Stripe's dispute id.** The first version cancelled a payment's line with one reversing line and treated any existing reversal as proof the return was already handled, so a second, separate partial chargeback on the same payment was ignored. It also dropped a return that arrived before the payment itself was on the statement, leaving the policy marked paid in full. Each dispute is now saved as a record as soon as it is heard about, and gets its statement line once the payment's line exists: at once in the usual case, or when the payment is recorded if the news arrived out of order. The same dispute heard twice finds its record and adds nothing; two disputes on one payment both count; the total taken back never exceeds the payment. A payment taken back in full is no longer a payment, and one taken back in part still is, with the returned amount noted beside it. Money the bank has taken back can't also be refunded.
- 2026-11-12 22:04 · **A failed refund stays failed.** Stripe never moves a refund from failed back to succeeded, so the app doesn't either. A "succeeded" that turns up after the failure is known (a slow answer, or old news) used to mark the refund succeeded again and post its line, wiping out the credit the customer was still owed. The refund's status is now read again under the policy lock before anything is posted.
- 2026-11-12 22:04 · **A preview is only shown if the form hasn't changed since it was asked for.** A preview for one date could arrive after the date field had been changed to another, and confirming then saved the first date while the field showed the second. Every input that affects the figures already cleared the preview when changed. It now also marks any preview still loading as out of date, so its answer is thrown away and the broker previews again for what is on screen.
- 2026-11-12 22:08 · **Returned payments are now tested against Stripe, with a card dispute standing in for a bank return.** A test-mode bank return takes days to arrive, but Stripe has a test card whose payment is disputed straight away, and a test-mode dispute can be lost on demand. Both reach the app the same way, as a lost dispute on the payment, so `npm run test:stripe` now runs a real one through the handler. The deployed endpoint is subscribed to the three dispute events the app acts on (created, funds withdrawn, closed). The two it isn't subscribed to aren't needed: "updated" changes nothing the app records, and "funds reinstated" means a dispute was won, which the app never posted in the first place. `npm run test:deployed -- returned-payment` checks the whole path on the deployed site, webhook included.
- 2026-11-13 08:18 · **Whether a payment was returned is read from the statement, not from its status.** A fully returned payment was recognised by the wording of its status note, and a late "processing" notice cleared that wording, after which a late "succeeded" relabelled the payment as successful while the statement still showed the money as due. Two rules replace it. News that a payment is processing, declined or cancelled is ignored once the payment is on the statement, because it has settled by then. And a payment counts as returned in full when the return lines on the statement add up to what was paid, which also repairs a payment the earlier code left mislabelled.
- 2026-11-13 08:18 · **A return written in the first, short-lived format still counts.** For about two hours returns were stored as a line reversing the payment's own line, with no record of the dispute. Hearing about that dispute again under the newer code would have taken the money back a second time. Such a line is now counted when working out how much of a payment has been returned, and reads as a return on the statement. We chose this over a data migration, which would have rewritten statement rows, against the rule that they are never edited. The deployed database has no rows in that format, so this protects only a copy of the app that ran that version.
- 2026-11-13 08:18 · **A confirmation's answer is ignored if the form changed while it was in flight and nothing was saved.** The same rule previews already follow. A refusal that came back after the date had been changed showed the first date's figures beside the second date, with a Confirm button that had nothing to send. The form is now left ready to preview what is on screen. If the confirmation did save, it is reported as saved, since it is the change the broker confirmed.
- 2026-11-13 08:40 · **Returned money has one source, and the first return format is retired.** This replaces the entry above about still counting returns written in the first format. The statement, the payment's status, and how much of a payment is left to refund all read the amounts posted on those linked lines and nothing else.
